## 1. TL;DR

- **A "bundle" starts at 3 properties booked together in the same area.** One or two isn't a portfolio — charge standard rates.
- **Recommended discount off Ben's standard per-bedroom rates:**
  - **3–4 properties → 10% off**
  - **5–9 properties → 15% off**
  - **10+ properties → 15% (custom quote), up to 20% only for ongoing/recurring managers**
- **The discount is only "free money" if the shoots are clustered.** It's paid for by *not* driving all over — same community, scheduled in one batched window. If the properties are scattered on separate days/cities, the saving disappears, so quote those closer to standard + travel.
- **The 10-property quote Ben already sent Katie (~$3,400 → ~$2,900–3,100, about 10–13% off) is right in the defensible band.** This SOP just turns that instinct into a repeatable rule.
- **Never discount below your cost floor.** Set a hard **minimum per property** (needs Ben's real cost-of-doing-business number — see Open Questions). Until that's calculated, don't go past 20%.
- **Collect a deposit before shooting** (25–50% of the bundle total, or invoice per shoot-day for big jobs) and get it in writing. Send the quote within **24 hours**.
- **Keep big bundles as custom quotes, not a public price grid** — bedroom mix and travel vary too much. The site now routes these to a "Get a bundle quote" inquiry.

## 2. The reasoning

A volume discount is not a favor — it's a **share of a real saving passed back to the client**. When Katie's 10 units sit in two neighboring communities, Ben drives once, sets up in a rhythm, edits ten similar galleries in one batched workflow, sends one invoice, and lands one relationship instead of chasing ten separate clients. Every one of those is cheaper *per property* than a one-off shoot. Handing back 10–20% of that is smart; it's the classic rule that a discount should map to a lower cost-to-serve, not to how hard the client pushes.

That single idea also tells you when **not** to discount. If those ten properties were spread across three cities on five different days, the trip-and-setup saving vanishes — you're doing ten normal jobs — so the bundle rate shouldn't apply. This is the guardrail most photographers miss: they give the volume discount but still eat the scattered-travel cost, and the "big job" quietly loses money. So the rule here is **quantity + cluster**, never quantity alone.

Why these specific numbers? Across many independent real-estate/STR pricing guides, the going convention is a **10–20% discount for committed volume**, with breakpoints around **5, 10, and 20** units. That's Tier-C practitioner convention (competitor pages and educators), but it's remarkably consistent, and it sits comfortably above Ben's cost floor at his current rates. The *floor itself* — the price below which a shoot loses money — comes from a Tier-A method (PPA's Cost of Doing Business): total annual costs plus a real salary, divided by how many shoots he can actually deliver. Ben hasn't computed that number yet, which is the one missing input; until he does, 20% is a safe ceiling and there should be a hard per-property minimum.

Finally, the reason to court these clients at all: a property manager with a portfolio is **recurring revenue** — new units, re-shoots after refurbishments, seasonal refreshes. Winning that relationship is worth a sharper price than a single stranger's one listing, because the lifetime value is many multiples higher and the marketing cost to win the *next* job is near zero. That's the real prize behind the Katie inquiry, and it's why a clean, fast, confident bundle process matters more than squeezing the last dollar out of job one.

## 3. Full research

### 3.1 The economics — where a bundle discount actually comes from

A defensible discount is funded by a measurable reduction in cost-to-serve. For a clustered multi-property STR shoot, the savings are:

1. **Route density / travel & mobilization.** Travel and per-shoot setup are a large share of a single shoot's overhead. Ten properties in one or two adjacent communities collapse ten mobilizations into one trip. Scheduling shoots by neighborhood to save travel time is explicitly cited as the mechanism behind volume pricing [C]. *This is the largest and most contingent saving — it exists only if the properties are geographically clustered and batch-scheduled.*
2. **Batch editing efficiency.** Editing ten similar vacation-rental galleries in one consistent workflow (same look, same presets, one delivery push) is faster per property than ten separate jobs [C].
3. **One transaction, one client (lower acquisition + admin cost).** Acquiring one 10-property client is far cheaper than acquiring ten separate clients — one inquiry, one quote, one contract, one invoice. (Principle 10: customer-acquisition cost; Principle 8: systematize the repeatable.)
4. **Recurring / portfolio lifetime value.** Property managers rebook (new units, post-refurb re-shoots, seasonal refreshes). High lifetime value justifies a sharper price to win the relationship (Principles 5, 7, 10).

**Corollary (the guardrail):** savings #1–2 evaporate if the properties are *not* clustered or batch-scheduled. A discount must therefore be **contingent on route density**, not granted on raw count alone.

### 3.2 What the market does (volume-discount convention)

Repeated, independently, across multiple pricing guides and practitioner pages:

- **10–20% discount for committed volume** is the standard band [C, corroborated ×4+].
- **Breakpoints cluster at 5 / 10 / 20 units**; ~5 listings/month is commonly "enough to trigger package pricing" [C].
- **STR/portfolio owners expect multi-property discounts** — "most companies offer sizable discounts for 2+ properties" in the STR-services world generally [C].
- **Retainer / subscription** (a set monthly fee covering N shoots) is the standard structure for *ongoing high-volume* clients — predictable cost + availability for the client, recurring revenue for the photographer [C]. This is the natural evolution of a 20+/month or repeat relationship.

Caveat per the skill's rules: these percentages are **Tier-C practitioner convention**, not Tier-A benchmark data — there is no PPA "official volume-discount table." Their value is in the *consistency* across independent sources. The **floor** they must respect is Tier A.

### 3.3 The floor (Tier A) and the value ceiling (Tier B)

- **Floor — PPA Cost of Doing Business.** Your price floor must cover overhead, taxes, gear replacement, *and* a real salary, divided by the shoots you can actually book. "Every rate below that floor represents a loss, regardless of how reasonable it looks on a price list." [A, PPA]. **Ben has not computed his CODB** — flagged as the key open question. The discount ceiling (20%) and a per-property minimum exist to protect this floor in its absence.
- **Value ceiling — the images make the client money.** Airbnb's own study: listings with professional photos earn ~**40% more** and book ~**24% more** than others in their area [B/C — vendor study, widely cited]. This is why value-based pricing holds even at volume: you're selling the booking uplift on ten units, not ten hours of labor (Principle 2). It also means Ben should resist over-discounting — the deliverable is high-value, not a commodity.

### 3.4 Candidate structures (vs. the Principle Lens)

| Option | What it is | Honors | Strains | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Flat % off standard, tiered by count** (recommended) | 10% (3–4), 15% (5–9), 15–20% (10+) off his per-bedroom rates | 1 (floor-aware), 2 (keeps value framing), 8 (repeatable), 7 (win recurring) | Needs the cluster guardrail or margin leaks | **Recommended** — simple, transparent, maps to his existing per-bedroom rates |
| **Flat per-property rate** (e.g. "$300/unit for 10+") | One blended number regardless of bedroom | 8 (dead simple) | 1, 2 — ignores bedroom mix; underprices big units, overprices small | Rejected — his value scales with bedroom count; blending discards that |
| **Custom quote every time, no published rule** | Quote each bundle from scratch | 2 | 8, 10 — not repeatable, slow, inconsistent | Rejected as the *rule*, but **kept as the delivery mechanism** for 5+ (see below) |
| **Retainer / subscription** | Monthly fee for N shoots | 7 (cash flow), 5, 10 | Overkill for a one-time 10-unit job | **Defer** — the right answer for *ongoing* managers (20+/yr or monthly), not first contact |

**Synthesis:** use the tiered % rule as the *math*, delivered via a *custom quote* for 5+ properties (so bedroom mix + travel are confirmed), and graduate genuinely recurring managers to a *retainer* conversation.

### 3.5 Worked math against Ben's current rates

Standard vacation-rental rates: **1–2 BR $265 · 3–4 BR $335 · 4–5 BR $425** (editing + 24–48 hr delivery included).

- **Katie, 10× 3–4 BR:** standard 10 × $335 = **$3,350**.
  - 10% → $3,015 · 12% → $2,948 · 15% → $2,848.
  - Ben quoted ~$2,900–3,100 = **~10–13% off** → in-band, slightly under the 15% tier. ✅
- **Mixed 10-unit example** (4× 1–2BR + 5× 3–4BR + 1× 4–5BR): standard = $1,060 + $1,675 + $425 = **$3,160**. 15% → **$2,686**.
- **Small bundle, 3× 3–4BR:** standard $1,005. 10% → **$905** (saves the client ~$100; costs Ben little because it's one trip).

At 15%, a 3–4 BR unit nets **$285** vs. $335 standard — still well above a plausible cost floor for a clustered shoot. At 20% it nets $268; below that, margin depends entirely on the (uncomputed) CODB floor — hence 20% as the ceiling.

### 3.6 Deposit, payment & legal (Principles 4 & 7)

- **Get it in writing before shoot day.** A signed agreement + deposit is the line between a business and a hobby that loses money on cancellations (Principle 4). *This is a business practice, not legal advice — for contract enforceability, use a reviewed template / attorney.*
- **Deposit options for a multi-property job:**
  - **25–50% of the bundle total upfront** to reserve a multi-day booking (recommended default), balance on delivery; **or**
  - **Milestone / per-shoot-day invoicing** for 10+ units shot across several days (bills as work is delivered — protects both sides on a big job); **or**
  - His standard per-shoot deposit × N (simple but front-loads a large sum on the client).
- **Cash-flow note (Principle 7):** a big bundle paid entirely in arrears is a risk; the deposit and/or milestone billing smooth it. Dubsado handles proposals, contracts, and payment plans for this.

### 3.7 Recommendation

Adopt the **tiered %-off rule (3.4), contingent on clustering (3.1 corollary), delivered as a custom quote for 5+, with a 24-hr response SLA, a deposit before shooting, and a 20% ceiling until the CODB floor is computed.** Confidence: **medium-high** on structure and the 10–20% band (strongly corroborated convention + sound cost logic); **medium** on the exact ceiling and per-property minimum (gated on Ben's real cost data). This should be ratified as a short **decision record (DR-000x)** once Ben confirms the numbers, then the published tiers can be finalized.

## Steps / requirements

**S-1. Define the bundle threshold.** When an inquiry names **3+ properties booked together**, treat it as a portfolio bundle; 1–2 properties bill at standard rates.
  - **Done when:** the "3+" trigger is stated on the pricing page and in the quoting checklist.

**S-2. Apply the tiered discount to standard per-bedroom rates.** When quoting a bundle, compute standard (sum of per-bedroom rates) then apply: **3–4 → 10%, 5–9 → 15%, 10+ → 15% (up to 20% for ongoing/recurring)**.
  - **Done when:** a quote's discount matches the tier for its property count and never exceeds 20%.

**S-3. Gate the discount on clustering.** When the properties are **not** in one metro/community or **not** batch-schedulable, reduce or remove the discount and quote travel separately.
  - **Done when:** any quote where properties span >~1 metro or require separate trips documents a reduced/again-standard rate + travel line.

**S-4. Enforce a per-property floor.** When applying a discount, the net per-property price must stay **≥ Ben's cost floor** (to be set from CODB; interim: do not exceed 20% off).
  - **Done when:** the CODB per-shoot floor is computed and written here, and no quoted per-property net falls below it.

**S-5. Deliver 5+ bundles as a custom quote within the response SLA.** When the bundle is 5+ properties, request bedroom count per unit + community, then send an exact quote.
  - **Done when:** client receives an itemized quote (per-property + bundle total + what's included) within **24 hours** of providing details.

**S-6. Require deposit + written agreement before shoot day.** When a bundle is booked, collect a **25–50% deposit** (or milestone/per-shoot-day billing for 10+) and a signed agreement before the first shoot.
  - **Done when:** deposit received and agreement signed before shoot day 1 (tracked in Dubsado). *(Business practice; use a reviewed contract template — not legal advice.)*

**S-7. Flag recurring managers for a retainer conversation.** When a client implies ongoing/repeat volume (20+/yr or monthly), offer a monthly retainer instead of repeated one-off bundles.
  - **Done when:** any repeat/ongoing inquiry is routed to a retainer proposal rather than re-quoted ad hoc.

## Targets & metrics

- **Discount band:** 10% (3–4) · 15% (5–9) · 15–20% (10+). **Hard ceiling 20%** until CODB floor is set.
- **Per-property floor:** ≥ CODB per-shoot cost (number TBD — Open Questions). Interim proxy: never below **~$225** on a 1–2 BR or the tier that breaches 20% off.
- **Response SLA:** itemized bundle quote within **24 hours** of receiving property details.
- **Deposit:** 25–50% of bundle total (or milestone billing for 10+); balance on delivery.
- **Legal & rights:** signed agreement + deposit before shoot day 1; usage covers the client's STR-platform + marketing listing use. *(Flag: contract enforceability = attorney's domain.)*
- **Track (Principle 10):** multi-property inquiry → booking conversion rate; average bundle order value; whether the client rebooks (portfolio LTV); lead source.
