Core plan — phase by phase

From decision to growth. Each phase cites the evidence behind it.
Phase 0 · before spending money

Decide & frame

  • Choose one niche by passion × skill × local demand × competition, and check it's durable, not a fad. Genres differ wildly (commercial > wedding/portrait; real-estate = low barrier + steady volume). Carty-Start 5:02 · Carty Top-3 · Jerad 8:00
  • Know your "nut" and work backwards — fewer high-value clients beat volume (50 × $2,000 = 500 × $200, far less work). Carty-LOW 1:18 · Sarah 2:14 · Katja 11:41
  • Pick a delivery model up front (esp. portrait): digital-file commodity vs. in-person sales of printed wall art/albums. Sarah "Portrait" 0:53–8:00
Phase 1 · skills + portfolio

Build the proof

  • Get reps — treat practice shoots like paid jobs; shoot friends/events to get comfortable with people. Cameron 4:28 · Sergio 4:16
  • Curate a cohesive portfolio of the work you want to repeat — quality over quantity. Carty step 5 · Tracy 1:53 · Kylee 2:25
  • Free-for-portfolio, never cheap — set the expectation, get a testimonial, then ask "what would you have paid?" to anchor real prices. Carty Top-3 4:35 · Cameron 5:59 · Cineminn 4:20
Phase 2 · money & legal Tier 1

Set up the business

  • Sole proprietor on the first dollar → Schedule C. No registration needed. Tiffany EA 13:12 · Nadia CPA 10:24
  • An LLC does NOT lower taxes — it's liability protection. Don't over-structure early. Jasmine Atty/CPA 0:55 · Nadia 46:04
  • Quarterly estimates + safe harbor (100% / 110% prior-year) to dodge the penalty. Jasmine 7:09 · Nadia 15:54 · Travis CPA
  • Track all deductions: gear, mileage w/ log ($0.72/mi), home office (exclusive use), software, education. Receipts 3–7 yrs. Nadia 20:46 / 18:20 · Tiffany 39:47
  • S-corp is a LATER move — only when profit far exceeds a salary (~$2.5–3.5k/mo); needs payroll + 1120S. Tiffany 18:54 · Jasmine 0:35
Phase 3 · systems before client #1

Make booking & delivery easy

  • Simple site + clear contact form (one-page Squarespace/Pixieset/Pixpa). Don't over-invest. Cameron 5:31 · Tracy 1:53
  • A CRM for packages, contracts, invoices, payments + the full templated journey set up before booking anyone. Kylee 7:42 / 9:50 · Eli 15:14
  • Payments via Stripe/Square/PayPal, not personal Venmo. Kylee 11:16
  • Welcome/pricing pack with boundaries stops scope creep; back up files in two places. Tracy 7:46 / 10:54
Phase 4 · pricing

Price for profit

  • Price for TOTAL time + full Cost of Doing Business — a "3-hr shoot" is 15–19 hrs of real work. Katja 0:59 / 3:49 · Tracy 3:32
  • Profitable from day one. Sarah's split: cost-of-sales ≤20%, overhead 30–40%, net profit 40–50%. Sarah 4:12 · Carty 9:53
  • Packages + anchor price + upsell at booking → higher average order value. Eli 5:04 / 6:24 · SnapHome 10:36
  • Portrait: sell printed wall art (in-person), not digital files. Sell the transformation + story; anchor to existing spend. Sarah "Portrait" 3:31 / 13:23
Phase 5 · marketing

Own your audience (owned > rented reach)

  • Blog every shoot with local + venue SEO — "searchable is profitable," compounds for years. Fix H1 / title tags / category pages / schema. Kylee 11:58 · Joy 2:26 · Thin Angle 4:29
  • Build an email list early; social is a "hamster wheel." Kylee 15:14
  • Relationships & referrals > followers; network (BNI/Chamber), target the right clients (top ~10–20% of realtors). Sarah 11:07 · Eli 1:46
  • Don't cargo-cult ads/tools — Chris spent $650/mo on agency ads → 0 bookings; all came organically. Chris 4:47 · Nathan 12:41
  • Define brand values; qualify for FIT; say no. A "bad client" is usually a bad fit. Luke "BAD Clients"
Phase 6 · grow & protect

Scale without burning out

  • Diversify / niche-stack once established — AI takes commodity work; authentic human work is growing. Jerad 8:33 · Nathan 15:16
  • Outsource the bottleneck (editing) — labor arbitrage; AI tools slash post time. Cineminn 2:43 (~$0.40/photo) · Sergio
  • Hire a second shooter at capacity. SnapHome · CNBC-Grace 6:51
  • Protect your life from the start — boundaries, avoid burnout (volume kills margin and joy). Kylee 17:58 · Grace scaled 46→25 weddings
  • Persist — year one is often a loss; don't quit or take shortcuts. Nathan 9:51 / 22:02 · Cameron 7:39

Research findings — the source of truth

By theme. Citations: (Speaker — timestamp); tiers per the framework.
A · MONEY, TAXES & STRUCTURE

Tier-1 consensus

  • Sole-proprietor automatically on the first dollar → Schedule C. Tiffany 13:12 · Nadia 10:24
  • Self-employment tax = 15.3%; W-2 withholding doesn't cover side income. Heather 1:31 · Travis 1:03
  • Quarterly estimates + safe harbor (100%/110% prior-year); the underpayment penalty is non-deductible. Jasmine 7:09 · Nadia 15:54
  • LLC = liability protection, not a tax cut; don't over-structure early. Jasmine 0:55 / 4:26 · Tiffany 15:25
  • S-corp later, only when profit ≫ a reasonable salary (~$2.5–3.5k/mo); needs payroll + 1120S; backfires with financed assets. Jasmine 4:13 · Tiffany 18:54
  • Deductions: gear, mileage w/ log ($0.72/mi), home office (exclusive use → business miles), software, education, % utilities. No deduction for donations or everyday clothing; meals 50% & only with a business contact. Nadia 18:20 / 20:46 / 46:54 · Tiffany 39:47
  • Records: separate bank account; receipts 3–7 yrs. Sales/use tax varies by state; digital increasingly taxed; you become a collector. Nadia 24:18 · Tiffany 6:39 / 44:47
  • A CRM is not accounting, and don't over-tool a simple business — a spreadsheet/QuickBooks is enough early. Luke-Taxes 6:51 · Rebecca 2:11

J · MARKET REALITY & CASE STUDY
The industry is growing, not dying — AI only takes commodity work. Global photography ≈ $55.6B → $81.8B; weddings, real-estate, product & food all growing; AI can't capture reality. Median income ~$33k (75% part-time); wedding $40–100k+, commercial $50–120k+. Jerad 0:55 / 7:26 / 7:52
Case study — Grace Torres (CNBC), 23, Lakeland FL: $177k/yr; weddings start $4k, avg $4.5–5.5k; grew $0 → $500 → $1.5k → $7k → full-time; invested early in contracts + a CPA; scaled 46 → 25 weddings to avoid burnout; bookings now from referrals, not social. CNBC 4:02 / 6:51

Definition of done — for the new photographer

  • Niche + ideal client + monthly number written down.
  • Separate business bank account; income/expenses tracked.
  • 25–30% set aside for taxes; quarterly estimates scheduled.
  • Portfolio of target work built (free-for-portfolio + testimonials).
  • Profitable package pricing set; contract + deposit required.
  • Website + CRM + payments + templated workflow live.
  • One owned marketing engine running (SEO blog + email + referrals); no paid ads yet.
  • CPA consult booked once income is steady; S-corp deferred until the profit threshold.
